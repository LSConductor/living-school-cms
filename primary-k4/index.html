Kindergarten to Year 4

Many ways into learning

English, Mathematics, Science and Technology, HSIE, PDHPE and Creative Arts are the Key Learning Areas. Inquiry gives children a question to follow across learning. English and Maths are taught with clarity and practice, while values and whole-learner care are woven through the NSW curriculum.

Foundations

English and Mathematics, taught with clarity

Progressive and child-centred does not mean vague. Curiosity and engagement matter alongside clear foundations.

Learning to read, with support

InitiaLit is MultiLit’s evidence-based whole-class literacy programme in Kindergarten and Year 1, with a designated support guide. It combines explicit phonics with vocabulary, oral language and listening comprehension through children’s literature. MiniLit sits alongside for children who still need help with foundational skills.

Growing confidence with numbers

Numeracy groups provide a focused setting for counting, recognising and using times tables, measurement and understanding space. Explicit instruction gives clear teaching and guided practice; learning in context connects ideas with the situations around them.

“I notice… I wonder…”

Children’s senses become a starting point for discovery. Inquiry sits beside safe foundations: children learn about themselves, their relationships and their community, as well as the things they investigate. A core Primary question: “How do my senses help me discover things?”

A rhythm for depth

Six keystones through the year

The year runs as six six-week blocks — one for each keystone — across two 20-week semesters. Each block is carried by an inquiry question, developed with students to suit their age and revisited as understanding grows.

IdentityPhonics, oral narratives, telling our own story.
NatureDescriptive vocabulary; shared reading of natural systems.
Life & LivingProcedural sequencing; clear instructions.
CivilisationCollaborative storytelling; community perspectives.
PhenomenaCause-and-effect language; designing questions.
ServiceExpressive writing; framing a shared goal.

Term 1 · Identity

Beginning with “who”

Identity asks who we are, how we relate to others and what is around us. Children locate themselves in place and time, learn to name and respect the Custodians of Country, and consider how safety looks, sounds and feels.

English brings phonics, oral narratives and personal stories. Mathematics brings sorting, classifying and mapping personal space. History draws on personal and family stories. Geography begins with place, local features and Country. PDHPE includes self-management, identity, health, wellbeing, respectful relationships and safety.

Every year, the question grows with the child. Primary K–4 prospectus · Identity

Science, making & the farm

Learning with our hands

The Kitchen Garden brings children into a living food ecosystem. Soil, plants, animals and pollinators offer a setting for observing relationships between living things. Science and Technology is one of the Primary Key Learning Areas; the garden gives inquiry a living context.

The Stewart Farm

Chickens, gardening, weeding, fencing and cooking, alongside construction. Kindergarten joins Friday farm learning after Term One. Families are invited to a Community Day on the final day of each semester.

Onland learning

OnLand includes cooking, making, growing, performing, exploring and serving, with time for supervised creative play. Agritourism farm programs and coastal excursions support outdoor education and PDHPE.

Primary Thriver

Thriver begins with senses and belonging: shore sentinels, knots, buoyancy and Bundjalung seasons — science and story through the hands. Challenge by Choice: stretch into growth, not into despair.

The whole child

Movement, arts, play and care

Character and capacity receive deliberate attention alongside understanding. VAST — Values, Attitudes, Skills and Trust — sits beside academic learning.

PDHPE & sport

Identity, wellbeing, self-management, respectful relationships and safety. All classes have a timetabled sporting program each week. Swimming is a Term One priority. School carnivals include Swimming, Cross Country and Athletics.

Creative Arts

Music, visual arts and drama are core underpinnings. Expression and performance are woven through the week — courage in public, not bolted on at the edges.

Unstructured play

Room to choose, imagine and work things out together, with adult supervision and guidance as children learn to manage relationships and shared spaces.

When things get difficult

Reception is the point of contact for first aid. A quiet space near the office is available under supervision. Incidents are recorded. A restorative model focuses on understanding what happened, recognising how others were affected, and working towards putting things right.

Screen time, on purpose

Imagination first; devices when they extend thinking

Early childhood is kept for hands, imagination and the outdoors. A screen arrives when it extends thinking, and responsibility grows with the student.

K–2 · Screen-free

Hands, story, play and outdoors. When thinking needs recording, the teacher writes it down.

Year 3 · Short and supervised

Small groups, for keyboard confidence and the feel of an on-screen test, including NAPLAN readiness.

Year 4 · A Chromebook, with citizenship

Devices are introduced beside careful habits: documents, accounts, and how to treat other people online.

Individual learning support

Support shaped around a child

Children bring different strengths and needs to the same rich learning programme. Targeted small-group teaching works around a termly focus. When a child has identified learning needs, we meet with parents; an Individual Learning Support Plan is developed with parent input and sign-off.
