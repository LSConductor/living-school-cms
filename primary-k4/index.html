Foundations · classroom

English and Mathematics, taught with clarity

InitiaLit is MultiLit’s evidence-based whole-class literacy programme in Kindergarten and Year 1. It combines explicit phonics with vocabulary, oral language and listening comprehension through children’s literature. MiniLit sits alongside for children who still need help with foundational skills.

Numeracy groups provide a focused setting for counting, times tables, measurement and space — clear teaching, guided practice, and learning in context.

“I notice… I wonder…” — curiosity with a language, beside safe foundations.

A rhythm for depth

Six keystones through the year

The year runs as six six-week blocks — one for each keystone — across two 20-week semesters. Each block is carried by an inquiry question, developed with students to suit their age.

IdentityPhonics, oral narratives, telling our own story.
NatureDescriptive vocabulary; shared reading of natural systems.
Life & LivingProcedural sequencing; clear instructions.
CivilisationCollaborative storytelling; community perspectives.
PhenomenaCause-and-effect language; designing questions.
ServiceExpressive writing; framing a shared goal.

Creative Arts

Finding a voice

Music, visual arts and drama are core underpinnings of learning. Expression and performance are woven through the week — courage in public, not bolted on at the edges.

Creative performance and communication extend through dance, storytelling, debate, theatre and horticulture. The expressive dimension makes room for thoughts and feelings to take form.

Thriver · Onland

Trying, moving, growing

In Primary, Thriver begins with senses and belonging. Shore sentinels, knots, buoyancy and Bundjalung seasons connect science and story through hands-on experience.

Thriver brings weekly Onland learning, camps and challenges, and excursions into one progressive outdoor education programme. Challenge by Choice: stretch into growth, not into despair.

PDHPE · coastal learning

Confidence needs both challenge and care

All classes have a timetabled sporting program each week. Swimming is a Term One priority. School carnivals include Swimming, Cross Country and Athletics. Coastal and farm programs support outdoor education.

Science · Kitchen Garden · farm

Learning with our hands

The Kitchen Garden brings children into a living food ecosystem. Soil, plants, animals and pollinators offer a setting for observing relationships between living things.

At The Stewart Farm: chickens, gardening, weeding, fencing, cooking and construction. Kindergarten joins Friday farm learning after Term One. Families are invited to a Community Day on the final day of each semester.

Screen time, on purpose

Imagination first; devices when they extend thinking

Early childhood is kept for hands, imagination and the outdoors. A screen arrives when it extends thinking, and responsibility grows with the student.

K–2 · Screen-free

Hands, story, play and outdoors. When thinking needs recording, the teacher writes it down.

Year 3 · Short and supervised

Small groups, for keyboard confidence and the feel of an on-screen test, including NAPLAN readiness.

Year 4 · A Chromebook, with citizenship

Devices are introduced beside careful habits: documents, accounts, and how to treat other people online.

Individual learning support

Support shaped around a child

Children bring different strengths and needs to the same rich learning programme. Targeted small-group teaching works around a termly focus. When a child has identified learning needs, we meet with parents; an Individual Learning Support Plan is developed with parent input and sign-off.
