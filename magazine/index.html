The school’s public overview

The curriculum pages published for families do not carry a director’s letter. This chapter is the school’s public overview, in the site’s own words.

Where character grows, curiosity leads.

For parents and carers who want more than worksheets: a warm, rigorous school where character and curiosity sit beside serious academics — IB pathways, expeditions, and projects your child can talk about at the dinner table.

Welcome to Living School - Northern Rivers NSW: a co-learning community where childhood is cherished and learning is treated as lifelong - not a race to a score.

Living School homepage

The K–13 Learning & Thinking Continuum shows how your child moves from Belonging in Primary, through Being in Catalyst Middle School, to Becoming in Living Academy — with firm foundations in literacy and numeracy, structured inquiry, and genuine care for the whole learner.

K–13 Learning & Thinking Continuum

How we grow the whole learner: human strengths, honest habits, and learning that makes sense in real life.

What matters is the promise: we teach toward character and capacity as deliberately as we teach toward understanding—and we keep families in the conversation.

Growth in these human qualities is not a percentage on a report card. We use conversation, observation, and structured reflection. Tools like a simple profile over time can show patterns—they are meant to start conversations between student, family, and teachers, not to rank children.

Belonging · K–4

Inside each neighbourhood the year is the spiral the site already describes: six keystones, each a six-week block, taught through Connections, Concepts, and Context. Only Primary Identity is a full spread. Every other keystone is one line from the site, with the question the public pages actually give.

Our calendar is built for depth, not rush. The year runs as six six-week blocks — one per keystone — across two 20-week semesters. Each block is bookended by a one-week Orientation and a one-week Revision, with short holidays between, and is taught through the three threads: Connections (keystone themes) · Concepts (English · Maths · SciTech) · Context (project-based learning).

Design dimensions, not scores. The family page calls this the Holo lens: a way the experience is planned, not a mark.

Being · 5–8

08 · Across the neighbourhoods

Onland and experiences

Every Friday, Kindergarten (apart from Term One) to Year 9 attend OnLand Learning at The Stewart Farm. This program was initiated during COVID and replaced the push to have students learning online.

Onland Learning fulfils our PDHPE requirements via an outdoor education offering. Students will partake in termly activities based on activity-based experiences that underpin our unique Progressive Education: cooking, making, growing, performing, exploring and serving, e.g. surfing, swimming, hiking, fishing, canoeing, cooking/gardening, construction, sport, learning family group challenges. There is also a focus on unstructured play – with our students offered opportunities to play creatively while supervised by teachers.

Every final day of semester, we have a Community Day – where families are invited to our onland site to participate in performances, meals and challenges. This day is a way of growing our culture of respect and shared responsibility.

OnLand Learning, for parents

Thriver is Living School’s intentional outdoor education programme from Kindergarten through Year 10. It grows good character the way the best adventure education has always done: real challenge, real community, and real places.

Your child does not only hear about resilience — they practise it. They cook, navigate, collaborate, sit still on Bundjalung Country, and learn to look after people and place. Academics stay serious; they simply meet the world where the world happens.

Every Friday, the classroom walls dissolve. From volcanic basalt at Flat Rock to the mangroves of North Creek, students build place-based mastery — senses and science in Primary; agency, navigation, and crew roles in Middle School.

Character is not built in comfort. House Survivor, mastery badges (earned, not given), and the Solo when students are ready — rites of passage that grow independence with care. Challenge by Choice: stretch into growth, not into despair.

Private buses become mobile classrooms across the Northern Rivers — rock pools, farms, museums, galleries, Elders, and community. Students practise civic confidence and service: beach clean-ups, planting days, looking after Jugun (Country).
